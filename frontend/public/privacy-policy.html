School Management Information System (SMIS) Privacy Policy

Effective date: 9 September 2026   Version: 1.0

This policy explains how an institution operating SMIS should collect, use, store, protect and disclose personal data. The institution operating a deployment remains responsible for its legal obligations and should publish its official privacy contact details.

1. Who controls the data?

The institution operating this SMIS deployment is normally the data controller for information entered into the system. Depending on the deployment, a hosting or maintenance provider may act as a processor or service provider.

2. Personal data SMIS may process

Depending on enabled modules, SMIS may process student and applicant identity and contact details, parent/guardian information, admissions and enrollment records, attendance, assessment and examination records, academic results and transcripts, fees and payment records, staff information, hostel/transport/library records, documents and photographs, user accounts, roles, authentication records and audit events.

3. Children and sensitive information

Educational institutions may process information about minors and sensitive information such as clinical or health-related records. Such information must be collected only where necessary and authorized, with heightened access controls and safeguards.

4. Purposes of processing

Information may be used for admissions, enrollment, academic administration, attendance, assessment, examinations, progression, certification, finance, staff administration, institutional services, communication, safeguarding, audit, security, reporting and other legitimate institutional functions.

5. Lawful processing

The institution must identify the appropriate lawful basis for each processing activity under applicable law. Consent is not a universal substitute for another lawful basis and should be used only where appropriate.

6. Data minimization and accuracy

Institutions should collect only relevant and necessary information, verify records, correct inaccurate information and avoid entering unrelated private information into ordinary notes fields.

7. Accounts and access control

SMIS uses authenticated accounts and role-based authorization. Institutions should use individual accounts, least privilege, strong unique passwords, prompt account removal and periodic privileged-account reviews. Development credentials must never be treated as production credentials.

8. Audit and security logging

Security-sensitive actions may be recorded in audit logs for accountability, troubleshooting, fraud detection and investigations. Audit information must itself be access-controlled and retained appropriately.

9. Financial and sensitive records

Financial records, clinical information, identity documents and other sensitive records must be restricted to authorized personnel and must not be exposed through public reports, unrestricted exports, shared accounts or public links.

10. Documents, backups and hosting

Uploaded documents and backups can contain complete personal records and must be protected accordingly. Institutions should use appropriate storage permissions, secure backups, restricted access, restoration tests and documented retention schedules.

11. Sharing and third parties

Personal data should be disclosed only where there is an appropriate lawful basis, authorization or institutional need. External services should receive only the minimum data required for the approved purpose, and institutions should assess hosting, communications, payment and other processors before connecting them.

12. Retention and deletion

There is no single retention period for every institution. Institutions should maintain schedules for student, academic, financial, staff, audit, document, communication and backup records and securely delete, anonymize or otherwise dispose of information when it is no longer required and no retention obligation applies.

13. Data-subject rights

Subject to applicable law and lawful exemptions, individuals may have rights concerning their personal data, including access and correction and other rights recognized by Ugandan data-protection law. Requests should be directed to the institution operating the deployment and handled using identity verification and applicable statutory timelines.

14. Complaints

Privacy complaints should normally be raised with the institution's designated privacy or data-protection contact. Uganda's Personal Data Protection Office (PDPO) is the independent data-protection authority responsible for oversight and enforcement of the Data Protection and Privacy Act, 2019. Official information is available at pdpo.go.ug.

15. Security incidents

If a suspected personal-data incident occurs, the institution should contain it, preserve evidence and logs, assess affected data and risks, revoke compromised credentials, notify appropriate institutional and privacy/security personnel, make legally required notifications and document corrective actions.

16. Cookies and local storage

The web application may use browser storage or similar mechanisms for authentication/session state and application preferences. Users should log out on shared computers and avoid saving passwords in shared browser profiles.

17. Public reports

Only information intended for public disclosure should be published. Student names, admission numbers, results, contact details, financial information, photographs and other personal data must not be made public without an appropriate lawful basis and required safeguards.

18. Changes to this policy

This policy may be updated when SMIS capabilities, security practices, infrastructure, legal requirements or institutional requirements change. The effective date and version will identify substantive revisions.

19. Contact

For privacy requests concerning an actual SMIS deployment, contact the institution operating the system using its official privacy/data-protection contact details. For software or security issues, use the institution's designated SMIS administrator or technical support process.

20. Regulatory reference

This policy is intended to be read with applicable Ugandan data-protection law, including the Data Protection and Privacy Act, 2019 (Act No. 9 of 2019), the Data Protection and Privacy Regulations, 2021, and guidance from the Personal Data Protection Office. This software policy is not legal advice; the deploying institution remains responsible for its compliance obligations.
