# School Management Information System (SMIS) Privacy Policy

**Effective date:** 9 September 2026  
**Version:** 1.0

This Privacy Policy explains how the School Management Information System (SMIS) collects, uses, stores, protects and discloses personal data when it is operated by a school, college, university, TVET institution or other education provider.

SMIS is software. The institution that deploys and operates SMIS is responsible for determining why personal data is processed, configuring the system appropriately, providing required notices, selecting lawful retention periods, and responding to data-subject requests. This policy is a software-level privacy baseline and does not replace an institution's legal obligations or its own privacy notice.

The policy is designed to support responsible processing under applicable Ugandan data-protection requirements, including the **Data Protection and Privacy Act, 2019 (Act No. 9 of 2019)** and the **Data Protection and Privacy Regulations, 2021**, where applicable. Institutions should obtain appropriate legal or data-protection advice for their particular circumstances.

## 1. Who controls the data?

The **institution operating this SMIS deployment** is normally the data controller for the personal data entered into the system. Depending on the deployment and service arrangements, an organization hosting or maintaining the system may act as a data processor or service provider.

The institution should publish its official name, physical address, telephone number, email address and any designated Data Protection Officer or privacy contact in its own institutional privacy notice and configuration.

SMIS itself does not independently determine the educational purposes for which an institution uses student, staff, parent/guardian or applicant information.

## 2. Personal data SMIS may process

Depending on the modules enabled, SMIS may contain:

- Student and applicant identification information, including names, admission/assessment numbers, dates of birth, photographs and identification details.
- Contact information such as telephone numbers, email addresses, postal addresses and emergency contacts.
- Parent, guardian or next-of-kin information.
- Admission, enrollment, programme, class, academic-year and course-registration records.
- Attendance, examination, assessment, competency, practical, clinical, workplace-learning and progression records.
- Academic results, transcripts, certificates, graduation and alumni records.
- Fees, invoices, payments, installments, receipts, refunds, balances, accounting and related financial records.
- Staff identity, employment, teaching, attendance, payroll and administrative records where those modules are enabled.
- Hostel, transport, library, inventory, communication and operational records where those modules are enabled.
- User accounts, usernames, roles, authentication records, audit events and security-related information.
- Information voluntarily entered into documents, reports, communications or institutional notes.

Some education records may be sensitive or require additional protection under applicable law. Institutions must not collect or store information merely because the software permits a field to exist; data collection should be necessary, proportionate and authorized.

## 3. Information about children and vulnerable persons

Educational institutions may process information relating to minors. Such information must receive appropriate safeguards and must be processed only for legitimate institutional purposes and with the authority or legal basis required by applicable law.

Institutions should apply heightened access controls to sensitive student records, avoid unnecessary disclosure, and ensure that staff only access information required for their duties.

## 4. Why personal data is used

The institution may use information in SMIS for purposes such as:

- admissions and enrollment;
- student identification and record management;
- academic administration and course registration;
- attendance and participation monitoring;
- assessment, examinations and progression;
- academic reporting, transcripts and certification;
- fee assessment, billing, payment allocation and financial reporting;
- staff administration and teaching allocation;
- hostel, transport, library and other institutional services;
- communication with students, staff, parents/guardians and authorized contacts;
- safeguarding, security and operational administration;
- audit, accountability, fraud prevention and investigation;
- regulatory, statutory and institutional reporting; and
- maintaining the security, availability and integrity of the SMIS platform.

The institution should not use student or staff information for unrelated purposes without an appropriate lawful basis, authorization or notice.

## 5. Legal basis and institutional responsibility

The institution operating SMIS is responsible for identifying the appropriate lawful basis for each processing activity. Depending on the activity, processing may be necessary for education administration, a legal or regulatory obligation, a contractual relationship, a legitimate institutional purpose, consent where required, or another lawful basis recognized by applicable law.

Consent should not be treated as a universal solution. Where processing is necessary for an institutional or legal purpose, the institution should document the applicable basis and provide the required information to affected individuals.

## 6. Data minimization and accuracy

Institutions should collect only information that is relevant and reasonably necessary for the stated purpose. Users responsible for data entry should:

1. verify information before saving it;
2. correct inaccurate or outdated records promptly;
3. avoid duplicate student or staff identities where possible;
4. avoid entering passwords, authentication secrets or unrelated private information into ordinary notes fields; and
5. periodically review fields and records that are no longer required.

## 7. User accounts and access control

SMIS uses authenticated accounts and role-based authorization. Access should be granted according to job responsibility and least privilege.

Institution administrators should:

- use individual accounts rather than shared accounts;
- assign the minimum role necessary for each user;
- remove or disable accounts promptly when users leave or change duties;
- require strong, unique passwords and protect authentication credentials;
- never publish administrator passwords in documentation or source code;
- review privileged accounts periodically; and
- investigate unexpected access or changes to protected records.

A development/bootstrap credential such as `admin/admin123`, if enabled by a deployment, is not a production credential and must be changed or disabled before production use.

## 8. Audit and security logging

SMIS may record audit events associated with authentication, administrative actions, financial operations, record changes and other security-sensitive activities. Audit records are used to support accountability, troubleshooting, fraud detection, security investigations and institutional governance.

Audit data can itself contain personal information and must therefore be protected, access-controlled and retained only for an appropriate period.

## 9. Financial information

Finance modules may contain payment histories, invoices, balances, receipts, banking references and related accounting information. Access to financial records must be restricted to authorized users.

The institutional system currency is **UGX (Ugandan Shilling)**. Currency configuration does not itself determine any individual's privacy rights or financial obligations.

## 10. Health, clinical and other sensitive information

Where clinical, health, disability, safeguarding or other sensitive information is recorded, the institution must apply additional access restrictions and handling procedures appropriate to that information and applicable law.

Sensitive information should be collected only where there is a legitimate institutional need and appropriate authority. It should not be exposed through public reports, unrestricted exports, screenshots, URLs or shared accounts.

## 11. Documents, photographs and uploaded files

If document-management or file-storage features are enabled, uploaded files may contain personal or sensitive information. Institutions should:

- restrict document access by role;
- use appropriate storage permissions;
- avoid public links to confidential records;
- remove obsolete files in accordance with the institution's retention schedule; and
- ensure backups are protected to the same standard as the live system.

Photographs and identity documents should be collected only for legitimate institutional purposes and handled as personal data.

## 12. Cookies, local storage and device data

The web application may use browser storage or similar mechanisms for authentication/session state and application preferences. A deployment should not use unnecessary tracking technologies without an appropriate purpose and notice.

Users should log out of shared computers and should not save SMIS passwords in a shared browser profile.

## 13. Hosting, backups and administrators

An institution may operate SMIS on a local server, LAN server, cloud host or other infrastructure. Whoever has administrative or infrastructure access may potentially access stored data and must be subject to appropriate confidentiality, security and access-control obligations.

Backups may contain a complete copy of the database and therefore must be protected as confidential personal-data stores. Recommended safeguards include:

- encryption at rest where supported;
- restricted backup permissions;
- protected backup credentials;
- secure off-site copies where appropriate;
- periodic restoration tests; and
- documented retention and destruction schedules.

A backup must not be treated as harmless simply because it is not the live database.

## 14. Data sharing and disclosure

The institution may disclose personal data only where there is an appropriate lawful basis, authorization or institutional need. Potential recipients may include:

- authorized institutional staff;
- the student or an appropriately authorized parent/guardian or representative;
- government, regulatory, examination or accreditation bodies where required or authorized;
- professional or placement organizations where necessary for an approved educational activity;
- authorized service providers or processors supporting hosting, maintenance, backups or communications; and
- law-enforcement or other competent authorities where disclosure is legally required or permitted.

SMIS does not authorize users to sell student, staff or institutional personal data. Exporting or sharing data outside the institution must follow the institution's policies and applicable law.

## 15. International transfers and third-party services

Before connecting SMIS to an external service, the institution should identify what personal data will leave the institutional environment, where it will be stored, who can access it, and what contractual and legal safeguards apply.

External services such as email, SMS, analytics, cloud storage, payment systems or reporting platforms should receive only the minimum data necessary for the approved purpose.

The institution is responsible for reviewing third-party processors and services used in its deployment and for documenting any required data-transfer safeguards.

## 16. Retention and deletion

SMIS does not impose one universal retention period for every institution. Educational, financial, examination, employment, audit and legal records can have different statutory and institutional retention requirements.

The institution should maintain a documented retention schedule covering at least:

- applicant records;
- student records;
- academic results and transcripts;
- financial records;
- staff records;
- audit and security logs;
- uploaded documents;
- communications;
- backups; and
- inactive/deleted user accounts.

When information is no longer required and there is no legal or legitimate reason to retain it, it should be securely deleted, anonymized or otherwise disposed of according to the institution's retention policy.

## 17. Data-subject rights and requests

Subject to applicable law and any lawful exemptions, individuals may have rights concerning their personal data, including rights to receive information about processing, access their information, request correction of inaccurate information, and exercise other rights recognized by applicable Ugandan data-protection law.

Requests should be directed to the institution operating the SMIS deployment or its designated privacy/data-protection contact. The institution should verify the requester's identity and respond within the timeframe required by applicable law.

A request should not result in deletion or alteration of a record where the institution has a lawful obligation or legitimate reason to retain the record.

## 18. Complaints

A person who believes that their personal data has been mishandled should first use the institution's designated privacy or data-protection complaint process where appropriate.

Uganda's **Personal Data Protection Office (PDPO)** is the independent data-protection authority responsible for overseeing implementation and enforcement of the Data Protection and Privacy Act, 2019. The institution should provide individuals with the appropriate PDPO complaint information where required.

Official PDPO information is available at <https://pdpo.go.ug/>.

## 19. Data security and incident response

SMIS is intended to support secure institutional processing, but no software or network can guarantee absolute security. The institution must operate the system securely and maintain the underlying server, database, operating system, network, backups and accounts.

If a suspected personal-data incident occurs, the institution should promptly:

1. contain the incident and prevent further unauthorized access;
2. preserve relevant logs and evidence;
3. identify affected systems and categories of data;
4. assess the risk to affected individuals;
5. reset or revoke compromised credentials where necessary;
6. notify appropriate institutional leadership and privacy/security personnel;
7. make any regulator or data-subject notifications required by applicable law; and
8. document corrective and preventive actions.

## 20. Secure development and updates

Administrators should keep the operating system, PostgreSQL, .NET runtime, browser, SMIS application and supporting services appropriately patched. Production systems should not expose database ports or administrative services unnecessarily to the public internet.

Source code, configuration files, backups and logs must be reviewed for accidental disclosure of passwords, private keys, JWT signing keys, database credentials or personal data before being committed, shared or published.

## 21. Public pages and reports

Only information intended for public disclosure should be placed on public pages. Student names, admission numbers, results, contact details, financial information, photographs and other personal data must not be exposed through public reports unless the institution has an appropriate lawful basis and has taken required safeguards.

Reports and exports should use role-based access and should be treated as confidential where they contain personal data.

## 22. Children, parents and guardians

Where an institution communicates with parents or guardians, it should verify that the person is appropriately associated with the student before disclosing protected information. A parent/guardian relationship does not automatically authorize access to every record in every circumstance; the institution must apply its legal, safeguarding and access-control requirements.

## 23. Changes to this Privacy Policy

This policy may be updated when SMIS capabilities, security practices, infrastructure, legal requirements or institutional requirements change. The effective date and version at the top of this document should be updated when a substantive revision is made.

The institution operating the system should publish its own current privacy notice and inform users of material changes where required.

## 24. Contact and responsibility

For privacy requests concerning an actual SMIS deployment, contact the **institution that operates the system**, using the institution's official privacy/data-protection contact details.

For software/security issues, the institution should use its designated SMIS administrator or technical support process.

## 25. Regulatory reference

This policy is intended to be read alongside applicable Ugandan data-protection law and the institution's own policies. Key reference material includes:

- **Data Protection and Privacy Act, 2019 (Act No. 9 of 2019).**
- **Data Protection and Privacy Regulations, 2021.**
- Guidance and information published by Uganda's **Personal Data Protection Office (PDPO)**.

This document is an operational privacy policy template for the SMIS software and is not legal advice. The institution deploying SMIS remains responsible for determining its legal obligations and configuring the system and its procedures accordingly.
